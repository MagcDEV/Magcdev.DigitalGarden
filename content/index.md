Welcome to my digital garden — a study notebook for software engineering, built in public. It's also where I publish my own [[posts/index|essays and blog posts]].

## Start here

This garden is a place to train, not only a place to read. Start with one of these three pages.

> [!example] 1. [[wiki/go/go-leetcode-learning-path|Learn Go with LeetCode]]
> New to Go? This path teaches the syntax and the patterns together, in 12 stages. Each stage has the Go that you need, a template to memorize and solved problems with comments.

> [!example] 2. [[wiki/neetcode-150/index|NeetCode 150 in Go]]
> All 150 problems in 18 stages. Each problem hides its answer in three steps: the pattern, the idea, then a tested Go solution with comments. You guess first, then you open.

> [!example] 3. [[wiki/neetcode-150/study-method|The study method]]
> How to practice so that the problems stay in memory: fewer problems, repeated after 1, 3 and 7 days, one pattern at a time.

## What to do today

One hour is enough. Do the four parts in this order.

| Minutes | What you do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Write one template from memory. Compile it. | The stage page that you are on |
| 15 | Solve the repeats that are due today. | [[wiki/neetcode-150/repeat-tracker\|Repeat tracker]] |
| 35 | Solve one new problem. Then type it from memory. | [[wiki/neetcode-150/index#All 150 problems\|All 150 problems]] |
| 5 | Say the idea and the complexity aloud. Update the tracker. | [[wiki/neetcode-150/repeat-tracker\|Repeat tracker]] |

> [!tip] Only five minutes today?
> Do the [[wiki/neetcode-150/pattern-drill|pattern drill]]. It shows a problem, you name the pattern, and it shows the signal that you had to see.

## How this garden works

Raw notes, solutions, and reading highlights go into the [[sources/index|Sources inbox]]. An LLM compiles them into structured, interlinked **Wiki** pages, and Quartz renders everything as a browsable site. The wiki is the LLM's domain. **Writing** is all mine.
