# Reading list: AI engineering and senior backend

These books support [[wiki/career/career-direction|the career direction]]: a senior backend engineer who ships AI features in financial systems. The list was checked on 2026-10-27.

> [!tip] How to read them
> Read one technical book at a time. Apply each chapter to the proof project or to your daily work. At 30 minutes a day, one book takes about four to six weeks. So the first two books fit in one quarter.

## The core four, in order

| Order | Book | Why, for this plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI Engineering**, Chip Huyen (O'Reilly, 2025) | The map of the whole field: prompting, RAG and fine-tuning, evals, agents, latency and cost. In 2026 it is the most-recommended book for engineers who move into AI. Read it first. |
| 2 | **Evals for AI Engineers**, Shreya Shankar and Hamel Husain (O'Reilly, listed October 2026) | Evals separate AI engineers from demo builders. Use it for the evals of the dispute-agent project. The book is very new, so check that it is out, or read the early release. |
| 3 | **Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition**, Martin Kleppmann and Chris Riccomini (O'Reilly, early 2026) | The backbone for system design rounds and fintech work: consistency, idempotency, events and streams. The new edition refreshes the classic. |
| 4 | **LLM Engineer's Handbook**, Paul Iusztin and Maxime Labonne (Packt, 2024) | A production LLM system from end to end. The code is Python. Read it for the architecture, and rebuild the ideas in .NET. |

## Optional

| Book | When to read it |
|---|---|
| **Hands-On Large Language Models**, Jay Alammar and Maarten Grootendorst (O'Reilly, 2024) | When a chapter of *AI Engineering* feels abstract. Its diagrams show how a language model works. |
| **Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)**, Sebastian Raschka (Manning, 2024) | When you want the model's internals. It is interesting, but the job does not need it. |

## Career

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| **The Staff Engineer's Path**, Tanya Reilly (O'Reilly, 2022) | What Lead and Staff roles expect: scope, influence and written decisions. |
| **Never Split the Difference**, Chris Voss | Negotiation. Use it for salary offers, and for customer talks in an FDE role. |

## Skip for now

- Quant and derivatives books. See [[wiki/career/career-direction#What not to do|why the quant pivot waits]].
- Books only about prompt engineering. They go out of date fast.
- Self-published LLM books from 2026. Many of the top search results are low quality.

## Free, and short

Read these first, this week:

- Anthropic's essay [Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents). It is the most practical start for the agent project.
- The tool-use docs from Anthropic or OpenAI.
